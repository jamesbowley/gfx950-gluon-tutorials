# Gluon v9–v13 vs AITER on the 256x256 loss shapes

37 of the 38 shapes from the 2026-09-28 status update for [ROCm/aiter#5897](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/5897) where AITER's pick beat Gluon v9 with the same tile, re-measured on hipBLASLt 1.2.2 (ROCm 7.2.4).

- **Same tile:** every AITER pick uses 256x256x64: hipBLASLt `MT256x256x64 SK3` (stream-K) on 28 shapes, AITER's asm `bf16gemm_bf16_tn_256x256` on 9.
- **AITER vs v9:** median +0.1%; AITER is faster on 21 of 37.
- **AITER vs the best Gluon version:** median -1.6%; AITER is faster on 7 of 37. 3 have fewer tiles than CUs, where stream-K spreads K over the idle CUs: 1024x2304x16384 (+177.6%), 2048x2304x16384 (+46.0%), 16384x512x8192 (+6.7%). The other 4 are within 0.9%.
- **Best Gluon version:** v12 on 24, v11 on 11, v9 on 1, v13 on 1.
- **Noise:** the same kernels re-measured two days apart moved by at most 1.0%, so treat gaps under 1% as ties.

**Method:** one MI355X; per shape one process with the same inputs for every kernel (no bias); `do_bench_cudagraph`, 3 interleaved rounds, median; every output matched an fp32 reference. torch 2.13 with ROCm 7.2.4's hipBLASLt, rocBLAS and hipBLAS; Triton `gfx950-tutorial-v2.2` with llirSched. AITER's kernels were logged from its own `tuned_gemm.gemm_a16w16` calls.

**Columns:** TFLOPS per kernel. Waves = tiles / 256 CUs; K steps = K / 64. Diffs are AITER / Gluon − 1, so positive means AITER is faster.
